Why people switch from OneTab

A list gets long. OneTab is great at one thing: turning every open tab into a list with one click. After a few months that list is hundreds of groups with dates for names. Tabby saves into collections you name (Work, Trip to Japan, Reading) and finds any link with ⌘K.

Losing the list. Like OneTab, Tabby stores data in your browser, which a cleaner app or reinstall can wipe. Tabby adds automatic daily or weekly backups, so a copy is always in your Downloads folder.

Help with what's open, too. Tidy up walks through open tabs one at a time (keep, close or save with one key), Close the copies removes duplicates, and History brings back a window you closed by mistake.

When OneTab is the better choice

If you only want one click to close every tab into a list, use Safari, or want to share a list as a web page, OneTab does that well and is very simple. Tabby is for people who want their saved tabs organized and their open tabs under control.

How to move from OneTab to Tabby

  1. In OneTab, choose Export / Import URLs and save the text as a .txt file.
  2. Install Tabby and open a new tab.
  3. On the welcome screen choose Coming from Toby, OneTab or bookmarks? (or later: Settings → Your data → Import) and pick the file. Each OneTab group becomes a collection.

Questions

What is the best OneTab alternative?

It depends on what you need. If you want saved tabs organized into named, searchable collections, plus help closing duplicates and restoring closed windows, Tabby is a free, private option for Chrome and Firefox with no account.

Can I import my OneTab list into Tabby?

Yes. In OneTab use Export / Import URLs, save the text as a .txt file, and import it on Tabby's welcome screen or in Settings → Your data.

Is Tabby free like OneTab?

Yes. Saving, collections, search, Tidy up, Sleep and restoring closed windows are free. Pro ($4 a month or $29 a year) adds extra themes and a few power features.
